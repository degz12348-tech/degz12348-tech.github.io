08 / FAQ

What teams usually ask.

What exactly do you own on a project?

The AI layer end to end: prompt architecture, the pipelines that call the model, the integration points into your APIs and CRM, and the evaluation that proves output quality before it ships. On Sales Coach AI that's packages/ai plus the cue-evaluation harness.

I work well alongside an existing frontend and platform team — I don't need to own the whole stack to be useful.

How do you keep LLM output reliable?

Structured input/output contracts, role-based and constraint-driven instruction frameworks, and a scripted evaluation gate rather than eyeballing results. If output quality drops below the bar, the build fails instead of quietly shipping.

Do you work with non-technical teams?

Routinely. Most of my client work has been translating what a business owner wants into something a system can actually do. I also run a free weekly live training taking complete beginners from an idea to a deployed AI app, so explaining this clearly is a practised skill, not an afterthought.

Your background is automation and BPO, not a CS degree. Why hire you for AI?

Because the hard part of production AI is rarely the model. It's knowing which business process is worth automating, what the failure modes cost, and how to integrate with the messy tools a company already runs. Eight years of doing exactly that — CRM systems, funnels, support operations, SaaS onboarding — is why my AI work ships instead of demoing.

The engineering is real and inspectable: TypeScript monorepos, job queues, evaluation harnesses, containerised deploys.

What are you available for?

Contract, fixed-scope, fractional, or full-time. Based in the Philippines and used to working across APAC, EU and US hours. Email is fastest — I reply within a day.
